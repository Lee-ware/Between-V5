# BETWEEN — KNOWLEDGE

 
## 1. Space
 
**1. Which planet has a day that is longer than its year?**
 
A) Mars
B) Venus
C) Mercury
D) Neptune
 
**Answer: B) Venus**
 
A Venusian rotation takes about 243 Earth days, while Venus takes about 225 Earth days to orbit the Sun. So one Venus day lasts longer than one Venus year.
 
***
 
**2. Which planet is less dense than water?**
 
A) Mars
B) Earth
C) Saturn
D) Neptune
 
**Answer: C) Saturn**
 
Saturn's average density is lower than water. If you somehow had a bathtub enormous enough, Saturn would theoretically float in it.
 
***
 
**3. What is the tallest mountain in the Solar System?**
 
A) Mount Everest
B) Olympus Mons
C) Mauna Kea
D) Maxwell Montes
 
**Answer: B) Olympus Mons**
 
Olympus Mons on Mars rises roughly 22 km above its surrounding plains—about two and a half times Everest's height above sea level.
 
***
 
**4. Which planet has the strongest winds in the Solar System?**
 
A) Jupiter
B) Saturn
C) Uranus
D) Neptune
 
**Answer: D) Neptune**
 
Neptune's winds can reach more than 2,000 km/h, making its atmosphere one of the most violently windy places known in the Solar System.
 
***
 
**5. If you stood on Mercury, which would be true?**
 
A) The Sun would rise and set roughly once every 24 hours
B) A solar day would last longer than a Mercury year
C) Mercury would have seasons like Earth
D) You would see Earth's Moon in the sky every night
 
**Answer: B) A solar day would last longer than a Mercury year**
 
Mercury takes about 88 Earth days to orbit the Sun, but one sunrise-to-sunrise solar day there lasts about 176 Earth days.
 
***
 
**6. What is the hottest planet in our Solar System?**
 
A) Mercury
B) Venus
C) Mars
D) Jupiter
 
**Answer: B) Venus**
 
Mercury is closer to the Sun, but Venus has an extremely dense carbon-dioxide atmosphere that creates a powerful greenhouse effect.
 
***
 
**7. Which object is actually a star?**
 
A) Pluto
B) Sirius
C) Europa
D) Titan
 
**Answer: B) Sirius**
 
Sirius is a star—the brightest star in Earth's night sky. Europa and Titan are moons, while Pluto is a dwarf planet.
 
***
 
**8. What would happen to your weight if you stood on the Moon?**
 
A) You'd weigh roughly the same
B) You'd weigh about half as much
C) You'd weigh about one-sixth as much
D) You'd weigh nothing
 
**Answer: C) You'd weigh about one-sixth as much**
 
The Moon's surface gravity is about 16.5% of Earth's. Your mass wouldn't change, but your weight would.

## 2. Earth & Nature
 
**9. How much of Earth's surface is covered by ocean?**
 
A) About 30%
B) About 50%
C) About 71%
D) About 90%
 
**Answer: C) About 71%**
 
Oceans cover roughly 71% of Earth's surface.
 
***
 
**10. What is the deepest known point in Earth's oceans?**
 
A) Puerto Rico Trench
B) Challenger Deep
C) Java Trench
D) Tonga Deep
 
**Answer: B) Challenger Deep**
 
Challenger Deep, in the Mariana Trench, reaches roughly 10,935 metres below sea level. NOAA notes that the exact number is still an estimate because the seafloor is difficult to measure precisely. 
 
***
 
**11. Which is technically a continent-sized living structure?**
 
A) The Sahara
B) The Great Barrier Reef
C) The Amazon River
D) The Himalayas
 
**Answer: B) The Great Barrier Reef**
 
The Great Barrier Reef is a huge interconnected ecosystem made primarily of coral reefs and associated habitats.
 
***
 
**12. Which animal has fingerprints surprisingly similar to humans?**
 
A) Koala
B) Penguin
C) Crocodile
D) Elephant
 
**Answer: A) Koala**
 
Koala fingerprints are remarkably similar to human fingerprints and can be difficult to distinguish visually.
 
***
 
**13. Which animal is famous for having three hearts?**
 
A) Shark
B) Octopus
C) Dolphin
D) Crocodile
 
**Answer: B) Octopus**
 
An octopus has three hearts: two pump blood toward the gills, while the third pumps it around the rest of the body.
 
***
 
**14. Which animal can change the colour and texture of its skin extremely rapidly?**
 
A) Octopus
B) Gorilla
C) Zebra
D) Penguin
 
**Answer: A) Octopus**
 
Octopuses can alter colour, patterns and even skin texture using specialised structures called chromatophores and other skin cells.
 
***
 
**15. Which animal is capable of regenerating an entire lost arm?**
 
A) Octopus
B) Eagle
C) Elephant
D) Giraffe
 
**Answer: A) Octopus**
 
Octopuses can regenerate lost arms, although the replacement develops over time rather than instantly.
 
***
 
**16. Which is older?**
 
A) The first dinosaurs
B) The rings of Saturn
C) The first flowering plants

### Majority mode rule for implementation later
 
 
- **Never hardcode crowd percentages.**
 
- Store the answer choices normally.
 
- The user's selected answer is compared against **real aggregated BETWEEN responses**.
 
- If insufficient data exists, show something like: **“The crowd hasn't decided yet.”**
 
- Once enough real responses exist, reveal the actual distribution.
 
- The player should be able to see whether they **predicted the majority**, not whether their personal answer was “correct.”
 
- For questions with context-dependent choices, preserve the wording exactly; don't turn them into fake objective questions.

# ESTIMATION
 
**Core rule:** Give a numerical estimate first. Then reveal the real answer and the gap.
 
The best Estimation questions should make someone think **“I have absolutely no idea… but I’m going to guess.”** The reveal should often be surprising enough to stick.

### Estimation mode rule
 
The app should ideally **not reveal the answer immediately**.
 
Flow:
 
**Estimate → lock answer → reveal real number → show how far off you were → optional “That was closer than 73% of players” once real data exists.**
 
For ranges, the answer should be treated as a **reasonable estimate range**, not a fake precise number. Some questions are intentionally approximate because the real-world quantity varies.



# PREDICTION
 
Prediction should feel different from Majority: **you are not predicting what other people think — you're making a claim about what will happen.**
 
The important design rule is that every prediction needs a **clear resolution condition and a date/window**. Forecasting research also emphasizes that good questions need to be verifiable and unambiguous, while delayed resolution gives the mode its reason to bring people back. 
 
So Prediction in BETWEEN should have three layers:
 
**Predict → forget about it → BETWEEN brings it back → reveal what happened.**
 
No fake certainty. No “psychic” nonsense.

## Prediction mode mechanics
 
**Every prediction needs:**
 
- **Resolution date/window**
- **Unambiguous outcome**
- **Stored prediction**
- **Resolution state:** Pending → Resolved
- **Actual outcome**
- **Prediction history**

 
For personal predictions that cannot be objectively verified, BETWEEN should **ask the user to resolve them themselves** rather than pretending it knows what happened.
 
For external predictions, resolution should only happen from a defined, verifiable source/event. Research on automated forecasting specifically highlights **verifiability and unambiguous resolution** as important quality criteria. 
 
And importantly, Prediction shouldn't become a constant notification machine. The payoff is **“Oh shit, I forgot I predicted that.”** That's much more BETWEEN than repeatedly nagging someone to return.




RANDOM

Random should feel like opening a drawer and finding something oddly specific.

Not “What’s your favourite colour?”
Not generic truth-or-dare.
Not another disguised Would You Rather.

The strongest random prompts create a tiny information gap, surprise, or social moment without requiring a whole game round. That lines up with research on curiosity in games: uncertainty, social interaction, strategy and anticipation can all contribute to engagement.

RANDOM shouldn't always mean “answer a question”

For the actual mode, these can rotate between:

Question → Do something → Make a guess → Notice something → Imagine something → Send something → Make someone laugh → Tiny observation → Odd fact/premise → Question

That prevents Random from secretly becoming Pick One with different wording.

Also: avoid making every card “deep.” Random works because the next card is unpredictable. A ridiculous pigeon question followed by a surprisingly personal observation is more interesting than 100 deep questions in a row. The research on curiosity similarly points toward uncertainty and varied forms of curiosity rather than reducing the experience to one kind of prompt.
